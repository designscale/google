The problem I identified
Once AI closed the technical gap between designers and developers, a new problem took its place: any student can now produce a polished, professional-looking product, so the artifact itself or final outcome no longer proves who actually has the design expertise. That's the problem my Challenge Statement takes on: making that expert judgment, the intellectual fingerprint, verifiable and assessable.
The assessment challenge — "Obsolescence of Artifact-Based Assessment"
With AI-native tools enabling instant, high-fidelity output, the "final artifact" is no longer a reliable measure of student mastery. The real work — strategic orchestration, prompt architecture, and critical curation — often remains invisible, yet it defines a student's true "intellectual fingerprint." This raises a key question: how do we validate Human Creative Art Direction within complex workflows?

The Vision (in my words)

"I hope this fellowship serves as a global laboratory to explore a shift: From Artifact-Based Grading to Cognitive-Process Assessment."

Goal stated in the application: architect a framework that validates Human Creative Art Direction as the core value, protecting the student's intellectual fingerprint in AI-assisted workflows.

Key Quotes From My Application

"Students stopped identifying as 'non-technical' and began seeing themselves as 'Creative Technologists.'"
"AI literacy is less about teaching new tools and more about teaching systemic thinking."
"The future of design education belongs to the 'orchestrators,' those who can leverage AI to bridge the gap between imagination and execution."
"I wanted to move my students away from using AI as a simple 'image generator' and toward using it as a sophisticated architectural partner to build functional, logic-driven digital systems."
