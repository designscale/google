My Presentation (Work in Progress)

🚧 Work in progress. This is the holistic outline of the presentation, pulling together everything drafted so far. It's built for the Sept 17–18 In-Person Institute in Sunnyvale, CA, and will keep changing as Session 1 (July 15) and Session 2 (Aug 5) feedback comes in.

Slide 1 — Title

Christie Shin · FIT · Google Higher Ed Faculty AI Fellowship, Inaugural North America Cohort 2026

Cognitive-Process Assessment: Making the Intellectual Fingerprint Verifiable

"How might we use Google Stitch, Nano Banana, Gemini Gems, and NotebookLM to build a Cognitive-Process Assessment framework that captures the domain expertise UX/UI designers apply when directing, writing, and refining AI-assisted design, making the invisible 'intellectual fingerprint' of expert design judgment verifiable, assessable, and protectable as student IP, scaled as the global standard for design higher education by 2027?"

Slide 2 — The Problem

Once AI closed the technical gap between designers and developers, a new problem took its place: any student can now produce a polished, professional-looking product, so the artifact itself no longer proves who actually has the design expertise.

That's what my Challenge Statement takes on: making that expert judgment, the intellectual fingerprint, verifiable and assessable.

Moderate

Slide 3 — Grounded in My Own Institution

I checked this against reality with James Pearce, FIT's Technical Manager for Faculty Research Space & Emerging Technology, in a Kick-off institutional check-in.

What he confirmed: FIT has no AI detection tools in place, and no plan to build any. Policy is conservative; day-to-day practice in classrooms is not.

"It's the wild west out there."
Why this matters: if the institution can't detect AI use, grading the artifact is a dead end. Grading the process is the only path left, which is exactly what CPA does.

Slide 4 — The Intervention

A Digital Product Design workflow where students act as art director, copywriter, and critical-thinking decision-maker across Stitch, Nano Banana, Gemini Notebook, Gemini, and Gemini Gems, iterating heavily while keeping humans in the loop at every decision point.

It's tool-agnostic and account-agnostic by design: James's interview also confirmed FIT has no fast lane for adopting new tools and only a basic Gemini tier, so CPA has to work on a student's own account, regardless of what's officially licensed.

Slide 5 — The Verification Mechanism

Judgment is made verifiable and protectable as IP through periodic in-semester presentations, where students explain what they decided and why. Each presentation is dated, so the explanation becomes a timestamped decision record.

CPA also grades direct contribution, not just curation: an original sketch, or the edits that turned an AI draft into finished work. That's what makes the fingerprint protectable in the clearest sense, an actual artifact of authorship.
