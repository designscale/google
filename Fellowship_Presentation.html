My Presentation — the 6-Minute Showcase

🚧 Work in progress. Rebuilt on Aug 6 for the Institute showcase slot of about 6 minutes, down from the 15 to 20 minutes originally planned. Detailed format still to come from Google. Six slides, roughly one minute each, no live demo.
"How might we use Google Stitch, Nano Banana, Gemini Gems, and NotebookLM to build a Cognitive-Process Assessment framework that captures the domain expertise UX/UI designers apply when directing, writing, and refining AI-assisted design, making the invisible 'intellectual fingerprint' of expert design judgment verifiable, assessable, and protectable as student IP, scaled as the global standard for design higher education by 2027?"

Slide 1 — The Problem 0:00–0:50

AI produces a polished screen in seconds. The artifact no longer proves who has the expertise.

Open by quoting Google's own Session 2 slide back to the room: "If we only evaluate the final result, we cannot know if a piece of work has been automatically generated with AI." Then land it: my challenge is the design-school version of that problem, and CPA is the discipline-specific implementation of the process-based assessment Google is already calling for.

Slide 2 — Grounded at FIT 0:50–1:40

FIT has no AI detection tools and no plan to build any, confirmed by our Technical Manager for Faculty Research Space & Emerging Technology. If the institution can't detect AI use, grading the process is the only path left.

This is where CPA comes from. Not from theory.

"It's the wild west out there."

Slide 3 — The Workflow 1:40–2:50

One diagram, tool by tool. Human-led research → Gemini Notebook synthesis → Gemini and Gemini Gems for UX writing direction → Stitch for the first UI pass from an art direction the student already chose → Nano Banana for assets → Figma for craft, design system, and error-catching. Let the diagram carry the tool names so the narration stays short.

Slide 4 — What CPA Grades 2:50–3:50

Eight dimensions exist, but name only the split that lands in a minute: did the student catch an Attribution Error, a fabricated fact, or a Logical Error, a step that doesn't follow even from true premises? That language is Google's own, from REVEAL. Show the evidence trail for one dimension, not all eight. The full table goes in the leave-behind.

Slide 5 — Proof + One Project 3:50–5:00

21
Young Ones awards this year, 8 in newly created AI categories
1
Gold Cube, Art Directors Club, for AI Process
6
Students hired before graduation

Bunny Quest, by Kokona Akatsu and Haein Noh: the CT&D minors personified as bunny mascots, extended into posters, stickers, social personas, and an AI-powered quiz. The Gold Cube was for AI Process, not AI Output. The award was for how they directed it. Drop the 20 to 30 second before/after clip here, recorded in advance, embedded in the slide.

Slide 6 — The Ask 5:00–6:00

CPA doesn't depend on a licensing tier or detection software, so it works for any program facing this gap, not just the well-resourced ones.

  • Pilot sites in other design programs
  • Two faculty willing to score the same projects against the rubric, so inter-rater reliability gets tested
  • A conversation with Google for Education about the AI Pedagogy Toolkit, and about Futures Lab at FIT

Leave-behind on the table. Thank the room once.
