Explain how you inspired change (max 500 words) — structured across The Idea, The Action, The Response, The Impact, and Reflection

The Idea
As Co-Chair of the Creative Technology & Design curriculum at FIT, I identified a growing "Technical Barrier Gap". While my students were brilliant visual storytellers, many were excluded from the high-level digital product space because they lacked traditional computer science backgrounds. When generative AI emerged, I saw an opportunity to redefine the "technical requirement" itself. The problem wasn't that designers couldn't code; it was that the barrier to entry was syntax, not logic. I wanted to move my students away from using AI as a simple "image generator" and toward using it as a sophisticated architectural partner to build functional, logic-driven digital systems.

The Action
I developed and launched CT380: AI-Assisted Design, a pioneering course designed to test a "logic-first" pedagogy. I integrated a "vibe coding" workflow — using AI-native development environments like Gemini — to allow students to focus on system architecture and user experience rather than manual coding. To ensure this wasn't just an isolated experiment, I spearheaded the development of a formal AI-Assisted Design minor at FIT. This involved creating institutional frameworks for ethical AI usage, defining new assessment rubrics that grade "prompt logic" and "architectural intent," and building a public-facing gallery to document this new frontier of student work.

The Response
The response from students was a profound shift in professional identity; they stopped identifying as "non-technical" and began seeing themselves as "Creative Technologists". Fear of the command line was replaced by the excitement of building complex, data-driven prototypes. My colleagues and institutional leaders recognized that this wasn't just a new course, but a blueprint for the future of the department. This internal success led to an invitation to share FIT's model at the UAlbany AI Plus Annual Symposium, where peers from across the SUNY system sought to replicate our curriculum to bridge their own institutional AI gaps.

The Impact
The impact is visible in the Gallery of AI-Assisted Design. Outcomes are measured not just by the visual quality of the work, but by the functionality of the digital products created — many of which were technically impossible for design students to build just two years ago. Furthermore, the successful approval of the AI-Assisted Design minor serves as a permanent institutional change, affecting hundreds of students annually.

Reflection
Looking back, I learned that AI literacy is less about teaching new "tools" and more about teaching "systemic thinking". If I were to do this differently, I would focus even earlier on cross-departmental collaboration, as AI-assisted design naturally dissolves the silos between design, business, and technology. This process has reinforced my belief that the future of design education belongs to the "orchestrators" — those who can leverage AI to bridge the gap between imagination and execution.

Your Purpose and Fellowship Vision
Why do you want to become a Higher Ed Faculty AI Fellow? * — Written Response (max 200 words)

The Challenge: The Obsolescence of Artifact-Based Assessment
The central challenge in my role as Co-Chair is the obsolescence of artifact-based assessment, which now complicates how we define Intellectual Property (IP). With AI-native tools enabling instant, high-fidelity output, the "final artifact" is no longer a reliable measure of student mastery. The real work — strategic orchestration, prompt architecture, and critical curation — often remains invisible, yet it defines a student's true "intellectual fingerprint." This raises a key question: how do we validate Human Creative Art Direction within complex workflows? By applying 20th-century rubrics to 21st-century processes, we create gaps in both assessment integrity and the recognition of student-led IP.

The Fellowship Vision
I hope this fellowship serves as a global laboratory to explore a shift: From Artifact-Based Grading to Cognitive-Process Assessment. I want to redefine "rigor" as the human-in-the-loop logic that transforms AI output into protected, original IP. I aim to leverage the cohort's expertise to help FIT architect a framework that validates Human Creative Art Direction as the core value in the creative process. I want to learn how my global peers are protecting the human "intellectual fingerprint" to ensure our students lead a logic-first creative economy.
