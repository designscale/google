1. Problem Breakdown — the Socratic audit

LensGoogle's questionMy answer, in short
KnowledgeWhat are the verified facts?FIT has no AI detection tools and no plan to build any. AI produces a polished UI in seconds. Students on this workflow won 21 Young Ones awards, a Gold Cube for AI Process, and 6 hires before graduation.
AssumptionsWhat are we taking for granted?That the artifact still proves authorship. That process evidence can be captured without adding unpaid labor. That students document honestly when documentation is graded.
EvidenceWhere is the data?External validation from award juries and employers. Missing: an in-classroom control comparison and inter-rater reliability across the 8 dimensions.
PerspectivesWhat might I be missing?Students may read process capture as surveillance, though the one student I have asked directly (Thalia Merino, Aug 11) called it support, with one condition: the record has to be verbal, because a written trail can itself be AI-generated. Adjuncts carry the grading load. FIT legal has not weighed in on student IP. Employers may not yet ask for a process portfolio, though recorded case-study walkthroughs are already standard in design job interviews.
ImplicationsSecond-order effects?Process grading could reward good documentation over good design. It could also create the first defensible student IP record in AI-assisted design.
ReflectionHow do my biases shape this?I teach the workflow I am proposing to assess, so my pilot evidence is my own students. I need outside raters to test whether the rubric holds in someone else's hands.

3. Vulnerability Map

Updated Aug 12 from the Merino interview. Naming the blocks out loud reads as rigor, not weakness.

BlockWhy it's realMitigation
Faculty grading timeEight dimensions across a full studio section is more grading, not lessRubric templates, and the verification layer is critique, which already happens in every class. CPA scores a conversation faculty are already having rather than adding an event to the calendar. It replaces some written feedback rather than adding to it
Evidence capture burdenStudents must log prompts and decisions while designingBuild capture into existing deliverables and into critique, which is already on the syllabus. Gemini notebooks as the container, not a separate system
No detection infrastructureNothing verifies that a decision log is authenticLive presentations are the verification. A student who can't explain a decision out loud didn't make it. Thalia Merino named this failure mode unprompted and proposed the same fix: "I wouldn't recommend 100 percent written, simply because it's easy for people to just AI it"
Surveillance, as students experience itEvidence capture can feel like monitoring rather than support, and a student mid-semester has no reason yet to see the professional value in itKeep the record verbal and make it the same artifact the industry already asks for, a recorded walkthrough of your own decisions. Thalia only recognized the value after graduating, through portfolio reviews and job interviews, which means the framing has to be taught, not assumed. Measure trust between faculty and students as a first-year metric, because it is the signal that tells me whether the risk is real
IP and legalStudent IP protection is asserted but not yet reviewed by FIT counselGet a legal read before scaling. Frame the timestamped record as evidence, not as a guarantee
Rater reliabilityOnly I have scored against this rubricRecruit two faculty to score the same three projects and compare
Tool churnStitch, Nano Banana, and Gemini all change under meThe rubric grades judgment, not tools. Tool names sit in an appendix, not in the dimensions
