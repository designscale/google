In-Person Institute — Impact Challenge Showcase 🔘

Thu, Sept 17 (9am–5pm) & Fri, Sept 18 (9am–1pm), 2026 · 1195 Borregas Ave, Sunnyvale, CA 94089

"The definitive outcome of your fellowship is a high-impact project, a major institutional challenge either solved or proposed using Google AI tools, which you will present at the in-person Institute showcase."
⏱ The slot is about 6 minutes. Down from the 15 to 20 minutes originally planned. Detailed format still to come from Google. Plan for six minutes with no live demo, and hold a longer version in reserve for hallway conversations and Q&A. The Aug 5 Spark Session was almost exactly this length, so that script is the starting point.

1. Problem Breakdown — the Socratic audit

LensGoogle's questionMy answer, in short
KnowledgeWhat are the verified facts?FIT has no AI detection tools and no plan to build any. AI produces a polished UI in seconds. Students on this workflow won 21 Young Ones awards, a Gold Cube for AI Process, and 6 hires before graduation.
AssumptionsWhat are we taking for granted?That the artifact still proves authorship. That process evidence can be captured without adding unpaid labor. That students document honestly when documentation is graded.
EvidenceWhere is the data?External validation from award juries and employers. Missing: an in-classroom control comparison and inter-rater reliability across the 8 dimensions.
PerspectivesWhat might I be missing?Students may read process capture as surveillance. Adjuncts carry the grading load. FIT legal has not weighed in on student IP. Employers may not yet ask for a process portfolio.
ImplicationsSecond-order effects?Process grading could reward good documentation over good design. It could also create the first defensible student IP record in AI-assisted design.
ReflectionHow do my biases shape this?I teach the workflow I am proposing to assess, so my pilot evidence is my own students. I need outside raters to test whether the rubric holds in someone else's hands.

2. Empathy Interviews

  • James Pearce, Technical Manager for Faculty Research Space & Emerging Technology — read the report
  • 2 to 3 current or former students: does process capture feel like support or surveillance?
  • A chair or dean: what would adoption cost in faculty time, and who pays for it?
  • A faculty peer outside CT&D: is the rubric usable to someone who doesn't teach UX?
  • Optional, high value: an employer or alum hiring manager on whether a process portfolio changes a hiring decision

3. Vulnerability Map (first draft)

BlockWhy it's realMitigation
Faculty grading timeEight dimensions across a full studio section is more grading, not lessRubric templates, and the periodic presentation replaces some written feedback rather than adding to it
Evidence capture burdenStudents must log prompts and decisions while designingBuild capture into existing deliverables. Gemini notebooks as the container, not a separate system
No detection infrastructureNothing verifies that a decision log is authenticLive presentations are the verification. A student who can't explain a decision out loud didn't make it
IP and legalStudent IP protection is asserted but not yet reviewed by FIT counselGet a legal read before scaling. Frame the timestamped record as evidence, not as a guarantee
Rater reliabilityOnly I have scored against this rubricRecruit two faculty to score the same three projects and compare
Tool churnStitch, Nano Banana, and Gemini all change under meThe rubric grades judgment, not tools. Tool names sit in an appendix, not in the dimensions
